OUR STORY · 2022 → TODAY

It started with a folding table.

No investors, no grand plan — just a small team, a farmers market stall, and one belief: fresh food should taste like something. Here's how it grew.

Chapter By Chapter

The Story So Far

Pick a year to jump to that chapter. Each one changed what we do and why we do it.

2022

The Folding Table

It started with a folding table at the Saturday farmers market. Three bowls, one ice chest, and a hand-painted sign. We sold out by noon — and knew we were onto something.

That first season taught us the most important lesson we still hold today: fresh ingredients don't need a lot of help. If you start with the best, you barely have to do anything at all.

"We had no menu, no website, no plan. Just a bowl and a belief."
2023

The First Storefront

Eighteen months in, we opened our first brick-and-mortar location — 40 seats, one open kitchen, and a chalkboard menu that changed with the seasons.

We built it for the neighborhood. Long tables, natural light, no rush. The kind of place where you can see your bowl being made and talk to the person making it.

"We wanted a room that felt like a kitchen, not a restaurant."
2024

The Flavor Passport

We launched our globally-inspired menu — six regions, twelve signature sauces, one philosophy: every bowl should feel like a place.

Hawaii taught us restraint. Japan taught us precision. Korea taught us heat. The Mediterranean taught us freshness. Each region added something to our kitchen.

"Every sauce has a story. Every bowl is a passport stamp."
2026

Fifty Thousand Bowls

We crossed 50,000 bowls served — and we still count every one. No investor money, no shortcuts, no compromises on what goes in the bowl.

Today, we're a small team serving a neighborhood we love. The folding table is in the back room. We haven't forgotten where we started.

"Every bowl is a chance to earn the next one."

A Letter From Our Founder

Why We Do This

Maya Tanaka

Founder & Executive Chef

When I moved to this city, I couldn't find the food I grew up eating. Not the version that was supposed to be "authentic" — the version that was made by people who cared what the bowl tasted like. So I started making it myself.

What I learned in those early farmers market mornings is that fresh food isn't expensive to make. It's expensive to fake. Real ingredients, handled with care and served with intention, do more than a hundred shortcuts ever could.

Every bowl that leaves our kitchen carries that same idea. It's why we don't pre-make anything. It's why we cut the fish the morning it arrives. It's why we cook the rice fresh every single day — even when it would be easier not to.

We're not the biggest. We're not the loudest. But if you come in and try a bowl, you'll taste the difference within the first bite. That's the only promise we can make — and the only one we need to keep.

Maya
Signed · 2026
Values That Shaped Us

Four Things We Never Compromise On

Our menu changes, our team grows, but these four things stay the same — no matter what.

01

Fresh, Always

If it isn't fresh, it isn't in the bowl. We buy small, cook often, and never hold anything overnight that shouldn't be held overnight.

02

Small Batch, Small Team

We stay small on purpose. Fewer seats, tighter standards, and the same people making your bowl every time you walk in.

03

Honest Sourcing

We name our farms and fisheries because we're proud of them. If we can't say where it came from, we won't serve it.

04

Flavor Without Tricks

Real ingredients, real technique. No fillers, no artificial flavors, no shortcuts. Just food that tastes like itself.
